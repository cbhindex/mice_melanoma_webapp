Do BRAF-inhibitor–treated melanoma nuclei look different on H&E?

An exploratory read-out of four mouse melanoma whole-slide images (Group 1, WM2664, BRAF-V600E): two Vehicle mice (366, 364) and two PLX-4720 (BRAF-inhibitor) mice (365, 363) — one slide per mouse — combining UNI-v2 tissue-region embeddings with CellViT++ nucleus measurements. Each mouse is one observation.

⚠ n = 2 mice per arm — every comparison is preliminary and hypothesis-generating, not statistically powered. Click any question to jump to the evidence.

Q. Do treated tumour-cell nuclei look different at all?
Yes, slightly. Neoplastic nuclei are a little more elongated (spindled) in both PLX mice — eccentricity ≈ +1.5%, long/short axis ratio ≈ +1.9% — a candidate morphological correlate of BRAF-inhibitor phenotype-switching.
See the region-based nuclear-shape evidence →
Q. Is this a strong, treatment-proving result?
No. With 2 vs 2 mice, "both treated beyond both controls" is the strongest attainable result and corresponds to an exact rank p ≈ 0.17 — not significance. The effect is small and sub-visual; treat it as a hypothesis to test, not an established effect.
See the honest effect size and statistics →
Q. Did anything else change — size, cellularity, tissue make-up?
No consistent treatment change. Nuclear size, density and cell-type composition vary more between the two mice within a group than between treatments.
See the whole-slide baseline →
Q. Could the elongation just be a staining or segmentation artefact?
The shape signal survives comparing treated vs control nuclei within the same tissue region, and holds up under segmentation QC. The "smoother chromatin" texture trend does not — it is confounded by un-normalized staining and is demoted.
See the robustness checks →
Q. How was it measured, and are the groups even comparable?
UNI-v2 groups H&E tiles into tissue regions; CellViT++ segments and classifies every nucleus. We compare confident neoplastic nuclei within viable tumour and within matched regions, using the mouse as the unit. The two groups' tumours are broadly comparable in make-up.
