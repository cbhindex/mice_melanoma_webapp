Do BRAF-inhibitor–treated melanoma nuclei look different on H&E?

Clinical motivation. BRAF inhibitors (e.g. PLX-4720) are frontline therapy for BRAF-V600E melanoma, but tumours acquire drug tolerance — often through a phenotype switch from a proliferative/melanocytic state toward an invasive/mesenchymal-like state, which classically looks more spindled. If that switch leaves a fingerprint visible on routine H&E, nuclear morphology could become a cheap read-out of drug response. This page asks whether such a fingerprint is detectable in these 4 slides.

Design. 4 whole-slide images, one per mouse: 2 Vehicle (366, 364) vs 2 PLX-4720 (365, 363). Each mouse is one observation; the millions of nuclei within a slide are summarised per mouse, not treated as independent animals. Everything here is preliminary / hypothesis-generating (n = 2 per arm).

1 · Are the two groups even comparable?

With only 2 mice per arm, tumours can differ for reasons unrelated to treatment. Before attributing any nuclear difference to the drug, we check the tumours are broadly comparable — and this section also defines the tissue and the cells we measure later.

Terms used throughout
  • Neoplastic nucleus — a tumour-cell nucleus (CellViT++ PanNuke class), as opposed to immune or connective/stromal nuclei.
  • Confident neoplastic — a per-nucleus quality filter (not a region): the nucleus is classified neoplastic with model probability ≥ 0.70 and has a valid size/shape, so segmentation and classification errors are removed. ~98% of neoplastic nuclei pass.
  • Viable tumour region — H&E tiles (256 px) that UNI-v2 groups as living tumour sheets; excludes necrosis/haemorrhage-like and edge/background-like tissue. This is a region, not a per-cell filter.
  • Eccentricity — 0 = a perfect circle, closer to 1 = a more elongated ellipse. Each dot in the plots is one mouse.
If the two groups' tumours have very different cellular make-up, any nuclear difference could be secondary to that rather than a direct drug effect.

Because several clusters are >90% one mouse, cluster-level "treatment shifts" are unreliable, and whole-slide comparisons mix different tissue types. This motivates two things below: restrict nuclear measurements to viable tumour, and compare within matched regions rather than across whole slides.

2 · Whole-slide nuclear morphology — is there any signal?

The simplest analysis: pool every confident neoplastic nucleus on the whole slide and compare the two groups. This is the baseline the rest of the page has to beat.

Start with the least-processed comparison so we know whether anything survives at all before adding tissue context.
Across all confident neoplastic nuclei on the whole slide, do the groups differ in nuclear size or shape?
Every confident neoplastic nucleus (any tissue), summarised to one median per mouse for each measurement; bars = per-mouse PLX-minus-Vehicle difference as a % of the measurement. A measurement "separates the groups" only when both PLX mice are beyond both Vehicle mice (exact rank p = 1/6 ≈ 0.17, the best attainable at n=2).
Whole-slide, nuclear size and most measurements do not separate the groups (the mice overlap) — they are mouse-specific. The one exception is a small, consistent-direction increase in elongation. But a whole-slide difference could still be a tissue-composition effect (the groups have different region mixes). That question drives Section 3.

3 · Region-based nuclear morphology — the candidate finding

To remove tissue-composition and slide-identity confounds, we now (a) restrict to viable tumour and (b) compare the groups within each tissue region separately — nucleus-level statistics, region by region.

Necrosis and edge tissue add noise, and clusters track individual mice; restricting to viable tumour gives the fairest like-for-like nuclear comparison.
Within viable tumour, are neoplastic nuclei more elongated after BRAF inhibition?
Confident neoplastic nuclei inside viable tumour regions. Left: the within-slide distribution of eccentricity, one curve per mouse. Right: the proportion of nuclei above a per-mouse-balanced elongation cut-off, one value per mouse, with the exact rank p and leave-one-mouse-out check.
The whole eccentricity distribution shifts up slightly in both PLX mice (not just a few outliers), and the same appears as a higher proportion of elongated nuclei. Eccentricity and axis ratio are the same measurement, so this is one signal, not several.
If elongation were only a by-product of the groups having different tissue mixes, it would vanish once we compare like tissue with like. So we test it region by region.
Does the elongation hold when the groups are compared within the same tissue region?
For every UNI-v2 tile cluster present in both groups, the PLX-minus-Vehicle difference in median neoplastic eccentricity (per-mouse medians; darker bars = clusters with 2 mice per group). The size measurement is shown alongside for contrast.
Because eccentricity is higher in PLX within every comparable region, the elongation is a genuine property of the tumour cells, not an artefact of the two groups containing different amounts of each tissue. This is the strongest evidence available at n=2 — though the effect is still small and not statistically powered.

4 · Is the elongation real, or a technical artefact?

Even region-matched, three things could fake a shape signal at n=2 with no stain normalization: whole-slide staining/scanner differences, segmentation errors, and the cells sitting in different neighbourhoods. Each is checked here.

Stain- and texture-based measurements react to how darkly a slide was stained and how sharply it was scanned; shape measurements do not. We must know which bucket the signal is in.
Which measurements can we trust at n=2 without stain normalization?
Each measurement is tagged shape/size (robust to staining) or stain/texture (confounded by un-normalized H&E and scanner focus). Eccentricity is shape-based; the "smoother chromatin" texture trend is not.
The elongation signal sits in the shape bucket (robust to staining); the "smoother chromatin" trend sits in the stain/texture bucket and cannot be separated from staining/scanner differences here — so it is demoted to the Appendix.
Two touching or fragmented nuclei can be mis-segmented into one elongated shape, which would inflate eccentricity for reasons unrelated to biology.
Are the "most elongated" nuclei real spindled tumour nuclei, or segmentation errors?
Crops of the most elongated neoplastic nuclei in each group, at native resolution, for direct pathologist review (segmentation QC).
Reviewer's call: judge whether these are genuine spindled nuclei.
If these crops are dominated by touching/fragmented nuclei, the elongation is partly a segmentation artefact and should be discounted. A blinded pathologist review of these crops is the honest way to confirm the signal.
A morphology difference could simply reflect the cells being packed differently or sitting nearer necrosis, rather than an intrinsic shape change.
Is the difference explained by where the cells sit in the tumour? (negative control)
For sampled neoplastic nuclei: local neoplastic density and distance to necrosis-like / edge-like regions, per mouse.
Local density and distance-to-necrosis are mouse-specific with no group separation, so the elongation is not obviously a neighbourhood effect — a passed negative control.

5 · What it means, and what would confirm it

Putting the pieces together.

Bottom line
Across these 4 slides, the only consistent-direction morphological difference is a small increase in neoplastic-nucleus elongation in both BRAF-inhibitor–treated mice (eccentricity ≈ +1.5%, axis ratio ≈ +1.9%). It is shape-based (not a staining artefact), it persists within matched tissue regions, and nothing else (size, density, cell-type mix, texture) separates the groups.
This is biologically coherent as a morphological correlate of BRAF-inhibitor phenotype-switching toward a more spindled/invasive state — but it is tiny, sub-visual, and rests on 2 mice per arm (exact rank p ≈ 0.17). It is a hypothesis to test, not an established effect.
    What would move this from hypothesis to finding
  • Blinded pathologist review of the elongated-nucleus crops (Section 4) to confirm they are real spindled nuclei.
  • More mice per arm to reach an interpretable p-value.
  • A stain-matched replicate (or stain normalization) before trusting any chromatin-texture signal.
  • Foundation-model cell-crop embeddings as an independent, model-free cross-check of the elongation (logged in TODO).
