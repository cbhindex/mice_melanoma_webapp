3. Does PLX associate with a different mixture of tissue environments?

MotivationA whole-tumour morphology difference can arise because the tumours contain different proportions of pre-existing environments.
QuestionHow much of each mouse belongs to C0–C4, and which environments are enriched or depleted in PLX?
MethodCalculate one tile-fraction profile per mouse. Report PLX minus Vehicle differences in percentage points.
Interactive result · each tumour's composition and arm-level differences
Interpretation
Methods & QC
The denominator is C2+C3+C4, which is renormalized to 100% within each mouse. These conditional shares are compositional and not independent effects. A positive C4 difference means redistribution within analysis-eligible cellular tissue, not an absolute increase across the whole section. Mouse—not tile—is the comparison unit. C0/C1 definitions and full-section context remain on Tissue environments.

4. Do neoplastic cells naturally differ between tissue environments?

MotivationIf nuclei naturally differ between environments, pooling the whole slide can hide or create an apparent treatment association.
QuestionWhen each environment is pooled across slides with equal mouse contribution, which size and shape features distinguish C0, C2, C3 and C4?
MethodUse tile-balanced mouse summaries as the primary view; show individual mouse dots so slide-specific patterns remain visible.
Interactive result · multi-feature morphology fingerprint
Interpretation
Methods, pooling choices & classifier status
Equal-mouse/tile-balanced summaries are primary. Literal all-nuclei pooling is descriptive only because slides contribute very different cell counts. Axis ratio and equivalent diameter are re-expressions of eccentricity and area, not independent confirmation.
