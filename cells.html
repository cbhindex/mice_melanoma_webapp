What do the nuclei look like before tissue context is considered?

1. Does the whole-tumour cell mixture differ between mice?

MotivationA tumour can look different because the proportions of neoplastic and non-neoplastic cells differ.
QuestionWhat fraction of all detected nuclei belongs to each broad CellViT class in each mouse?
MethodCount all detected nuclei within each WSI, collapse CellViT labels into three broad classes, and divide by that mouse's total. Each bar is one mouse.
Interactive result
InterpretationDifferences among these four mouse bars describe whole-slide cellular composition. They do not establish a treatment effect, and they do not identify which tissue environment contains those cells.

2. Does whole-tumour nuclear density differ between Vehicle and PLX?

MotivationA change in cell abundance can accompany altered tumour architecture even when individual nuclear shapes do not change.
QuestionWithin each broad cell class, are nuclei more or less dense in the two PLX slides than in the two Vehicle slides?
MethodDivide each mouse's class count by its estimated tissue area. The panels show all four mouse-level values; nuclei are not treated as independent treatment replicates.
Interactive result · one dot = one mouse
InterpretationRead the separation and overlap of the four dots, not an arm-average bar. With two mice per arm and one slide per mouse, apparent arm separation may still be animal, section, stain or segmentation variation.

3. Do whole-slide nuclear measurements differ between Vehicle and PLX?

MotivationThe main biological question concerns neoplastic-cell morphology, but an unstratified view is useful as a baseline before matching tissue context.
QuestionFor the selected nuclear measurement, where do the four mouse-level medians lie within each CellViT class?
MethodWithin each mouse and cell class, take the median from the displayed nucleus subsample. The treatment comparison therefore contains four dots, not thousands of pseudo-replicate nuclei.
Interactive result · one dot = one mouse
InterpretationThis is deliberately a whole-slide baseline. A credible PLX-associated neoplastic phenotype must also be examined within like-for-like tissue environments. Use the linked context-aware pages before drawing a biological conclusion.
